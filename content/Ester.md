In the context of hormones, you can think of this as a packaging/carrier molecule. Yes, you can take [[Estradiol|E2]] raw, but then you'll just get it all at once, and then it's [[Metabolism|metabolized]] and [[Elimination|eliminated]]. [[Estradiol|E2]] bound to an ester is inactive, and the bond between the [[Estradiol|E2]] molecule and the ester needs to be broken to activate the former. This is useful because this slows down the release of [[Estradiol|E2]]. The known esters of [[Estradiol|E2]] are: benzoate (EB), valerate ([[Estradiol Valerate|EV]]), enanthate (EEn), cyprionate (EC), undecyclate (EUn), in order of increasing [[half-life]].
